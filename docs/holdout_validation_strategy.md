# Universal Holdout Group: Validating the Priority List

While the **Retention Priority Score** mathematically ranks who we *should* target, we cannot prove the financial effectiveness of the marketing team's subsequent campaigns without a clean counterfactual baseline. 

If marketing targets the top 1,000 customers on the priority list and generates £1 Million in revenue, they cannot claim they "caused" £1 Million in value. Some portion of those high-value customers would have returned and purchased organically anyway. 

To measure the *true* incremental value generated by marketing interventions, we must implement a **Universal Holdout Group (UHG)**.

---

## 1. The 10% Holdout Protocol

When the `retention_priority_list.csv` is generated and handed off to the marketing activation platforms (e.g., Salesforce, Braze), we will inject a strict firewall rule:

*   **Randomized Holdout:** Exactly **10%** of the customers on the priority list (selected via a randomized hash of their CustomerID) will be tagged with a persistent `[UHG]` flag.
*   **The Firewall:** Any customer with the `[UHG]` flag must be automatically excluded from *all* proactive retention campaigns, discount emails, and targeted SMS offers for a minimum of 6 months. 

## 2. Establishing the Counterfactual Baseline

This 10% holdout group serves as our **counterfactual**—it represents exactly what the top-priority customers would do if the business completely ignored them.

Because the 10% holdout group is randomly selected from the exact same priority tiers as the 90% treatment group, they share the same predicted CLV, the same flight risk, and the same seasonality exposure. 

### Measuring True Impact
At the end of the 6-month evaluation period, we will compare the actual realized revenue of the two groups:

1.  **Treatment Group (90%):** Received targeted retention offers.
2.  **Holdout Group (10%):** Received no offers.

**Formula for True Incremental Revenue:**
$$ \text{Incremental Revenue} = (\text{Avg Spend}_{\text{Treatment}} - \text{Avg Spend}_{\text{Holdout}}) \times \text{Total Treatment Customers} $$

If the Treatment group averages £500 in spend, and the Holdout group averages £450 in spend, the marketing campaign's *true* lift was only **£50 per customer**, not £500.

## 3. Protecting Against "Discount Addiction"
Beyond measuring ROI, the 10% holdout group serves a critical secondary purpose: detecting **Margin Erosion**. 

If we constantly bombard high-priority customers with retention discounts, we risk training them to only buy when a sale is active. By maintaining a clean 10% holdout group, we can track if the Treatment group begins to exhibit lower gross profit margins over time compared to the Holdout group, alerting us to "discount addiction" before it damages the bottom line.
