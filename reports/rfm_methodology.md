# ValueLens: RFM Methodology Design

## Core Definitions
RFM analysis evaluates customer value by examining three key dimensions of their purchasing behavior:

- **Recency**: The number of days since the customer's most recent purchase. A lower Recency score indicates a "fresher" and more active relationship.
- **Frequency**: The total number of distinct invoices (orders) placed by the customer. This measures loyalty and engagement.
- **Monetary**: The total positive sales revenue generated by the customer over their lifetime in the dataset. This represents the direct financial value of the customer.

## The Snapshot Date
For this analysis, the "snapshot date" (or analysis anchor date) will be set to **one day after the maximum transaction date in the dataset** (which we previously identified as `2011-12-09`).

### Why use this snapshot date instead of today's date?
Using `datetime.now()` (today's date) on historical data is a critical analytical error. The UCI Online Retail dataset ends in December 2011. If we used today's date, every customer would have a Recency of over a decade. This would completely obscure the actual variance in purchasing behavior *at the time the data was collected*. Setting the snapshot to `Max(InvoiceDate) + 1 Day` effectively simulates running this analysis on the very day the dataset was exported, preserving the accurate relative recency between active and dormant customers.

## Why is RFM Appropriate?
RFM is a widely adopted, highly interpretable framework for customer segmentation in retail. Unlike complex black-box machine learning models, RFM rules are transparent to business stakeholders (e.g., Marketing can easily understand what a "High Recency, High Frequency" segment means). It translates raw transactional logs into behavioral scores that directly map to specific marketing actions, such as win-back campaigns for high-Monetary/low-Recency customers or loyalty rewards for high-Frequency buyers.

## Why Total Revenue Alone is Insufficient
Relying solely on `Monetary` value to identify "good" customers is a flawed strategy. For example:
- **Customer A** spent £10,000 in a single order 11 months ago, but never returned.
- **Customer B** spends £500 every single month and bought something yesterday (Total: £5,500).

If we only look at total revenue, Customer A is prioritized. However, Customer B is actively engaged, loyal, and likely has a much higher future Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). Customer A has likely already churned. RFM balances the raw revenue with behavioral health metrics (Recency and Frequency) to provide a 3D, holistic view of customer health.

## Assumptions
- **Customer Identifier Integrity**: We assume `CustomerID` accurately tracks a single entity over time without account sharing or duplicate accounts for the same person.
- **Purchase Intent**: We assume that every discrete `InvoiceNo` represents a distinct shopping session or purchase decision.
- **Time Window Consistency**: We are analyzing roughly a one-year window. We assume this is a sufficient duration to capture the typical buying cycle for this specific online retailer.

## Limitations
- **Ignores Product Mix**: RFM treats all revenue equally. It does not distinguish between a customer buying high-margin flagship items versus low-margin clearance items.
- **Ignores Customer Tenure**: A customer who made 5 purchases in their first month looks mathematically similar in Frequency to a 5-year loyalist who made 5 purchases this year.
- **Hard Thresholds**: Traditional RFM often uses arbitrary quantiles (like deciles or quintiles) which can force behaviorally distinct customer groups into the same statistical bucket.

## Potential Leakage or Bias
- **Wholesale vs. Retail Bias**: The dataset contains some extreme outliers (e.g., a customer spending £77k in one order). These are likely B2B wholesale buyers. Grouping them with B2C retail customers can heavily skew the Monetary quantiles, pushing otherwise "high-value" retail customers into lower percentiles.
- **Seasonality Bias**: The dataset ends in early December, immediately following the massive Black Friday/Holiday shopping surge. This means Recency scores will be artificially skewed heavily towards "Recent" for a large portion of the cohort.
- **Truncation Bias**: Customers acquired in November 2011 will naturally have low Frequency and Monetary scores simply because they haven't had time to mature. The basic RFM framework might misclassify them as "low value" rather than "new".
